# Milestone 34: Vehicles in transit

Status: in progress. It continues the vehicle work from OLC-7 (see `OLC.md`) and
the OLC arc, so it is Milestone 34 in the project sequence. Slice 1 (timed
transit), slice 2a (a path with pass-through rooms), and slice 2b (windows into
adjacent rooms) have shipped; slice 2c (elevators on a path) is planned.

## Goal

A vehicle today hops from stop to stop with no time in between: the move is one
step, so a train, elevator, boat, or car is at its origin one instant and its
destination the next. This milestone gives travel duration and, eventually, a
sense of place while under way.

Two phases, matching how much the engine can support now:

1. **Timed transit (build now).** A vehicle spends real game time between stops.
   It departs, is under way for a while with its doors closed, then arrives.
   Riders feel the trip; a platform sees the vehicle leave and, later, the next
   one pull in. This needs no new spatial model.
2. **Spatial awareness (deferred, the eventual vision).** A vehicle knows where
   it is in the world while under way, so it can show what is outside: from
   inside a subway you glimpse a platform or a tunnel as you pass, and the
   players standing there, and they see the train go by. This needs a path or
   segment model and a windowing mechanism, so it is designed here but gated on
   that groundwork.

## Slice 1: Timed transit (shipped)

### The runtime enabler: a per-cycle dwell

`verbmain`'s event loop read `verb_dwell()` once at startup and reused that fixed
period for the agent's life. A vehicle needs two periods, a long wait at a stop
and a short run between stops, so the loop now calls `verb_dwell()` before each
block. Any agent can therefore vary its tick with its state (a mob that speeds
up when it gives chase, a vehicle that alternates wait and run). This is
backward compatible: an agent that returns a constant is unaffected, and the
`dwell <= 0` "block until an event" rule is applied each cycle as before.

### The transit state

A vehicle gains one optional property, `transit` (milliseconds of travel time).
Unset or `<= 0` keeps the old instant hop, so existing vehicles are unchanged.
With a positive `transit` the agent runs a two-phase cycle:

- **Depart.** When it is time to move (a train's stop dwell elapsed, or an
  elevator's `floor`/`call` request), the agent announces departure to the
  riders and the origin platform, moves itself to the target stop, and sets a
  `moving` flag. It is now at the destination but closed, doors shut, still
  under way. (`sys_move` needs a real destination, so the vehicle rides at the
  target stop rather than nowhere; a true off-map limbo waits for slice 2's
  segment model.)
- **Arrive.** After `transit` ms (the agent's dwell while `moving`), the agent
  clears `moving` and announces arrival to the riders and the destination
  platform: the doors open.

`verb_dwell()` returns `transit` while `moving` and the normal `dwell` (or block,
for an elevator) while stopped, which the per-cycle read above makes possible.

### Doors closed while under way

While `moving` is set the vehicle's controls refuse, so a closed vehicle carries
its riders and takes no one on or off: `board`, `disembark`, `floor`, and `call`
each decline with a "doors are closed" line until the vehicle arrives. A rider
is carried along and cannot get off in the middle. (Stepping off into a tunnel
is a gameplay flourish left for later, and pairs naturally with slice 2's
segment model.)

### Verification

Shipped: `verbmain` re-reads `verb_dwell()` each cycle; `agent_vehicle.c` has a
state-aware `verb_dwell` (`transit` while `moving`, else `dwell`) and a two-phase
`go_to`/`open_doors`; and `verb_vehicle.c` refuses `board`, `disembark`, `floor`,
and `call` while `moving`. A smoke test gives the lift a `transit` time, rides
it, confirms a disembark is refused with "doors are closed" while under way and
that it arrives at the target stop after the delay, then that a rider can step
off once stopped. Vehicles with no `transit` still hop in one step, so the
existing OLC-7 tests pass unchanged.

## Slice 2a: A path with pass-through rooms (shipped)

The eventual vision: while under way a vehicle is somewhere, not nowhere, and
that somewhere is observable both ways. Slice 2a delivers the first, simplest
form of that, for a self-paced train.

### A path, not a jump

A train may carry a `path` property alongside `route`: a comma-separated list of
every room it traverses in order, the stops among them plus the pass-through
rooms between (a tunnel, a stretch of track or water). With a `path` the train
walks one room per tick instead of hopping stop to stop, so "where is the train"
has a real answer at every tick. `route` still names the stops, so `board`,
`floor`, and `call` are unchanged; a room in `path` that is also in `route` is a
stop, and any other is a pass-through. `path_idx` tracks the train's place, and
each tick re-derives it from the train's actual location, so a builder can place
the train anywhere on its path and it stays in step.

Only a self-paced train (a positive `dwell`) follows a path. An on-command
elevator still hops directly to the requested stop; giving it a path (so it shows
the floors it passes) is a later sub-slice, since it must walk toward an
arbitrary target rather than loop.

### The vehicle occupies each room

The train really moves into each room on its path, pass-through rooms included,
so its position is a plain fact any verb can read: `@contents` of a tunnel shows
the train while it is there. At a stop it clears `moving`, so the doors open and
it waits its `dwell`; in a pass-through it sets `moving`, so the doors stay shut
(slice 1's controls still refuse) and it waits its `transit` time before the next
step. Visibility is then just a broadcast to the room it occupies, no separate
"what can this segment see" model.

### Windows

While the train passes through a room that is not a stop, both sides see each
other. The room hears the train go by ("The subway rushes past."). The riders
see where they are ("Through the window: the tunnel.") and, for each player
standing in that room, a glimpse of them by name ("You glimpse Ada outside."). A
player avatar is told from the train itself and from loose items by its `charid`,
so only people are named, not scenery.

### Verification

Shipped: `agent_vehicle.c` reads an optional `path`, and for a train with one it
walks the rooms in turn (`advance_path`), opening the doors at a stop and running
a `window` broadcast at a pass-through. A smoke test builds a tunnel between two
stops, rides a looping subway through it (the rider sees the tunnel go by, then
the arrival at the next stop), then steps off into the tunnel and sees the subway
rush past. A train with no `path` still hops stop to stop, so slice 1 and the
OLC-7 tests pass unchanged.

## Slice 2b: Windows into adjacent rooms (shipped)

Slice 2a lets a rider and a person in the same pass-through room see each other.
Slice 2b adds sight between rooms that the train does not enter: a rider glimpses
a platform the train skips past but never stops at, or a person in a tunnel
alongside the track rather than on it, and they see the train pass in the
distance.

A pass-through room may carry an `observe` property: a comma-separated list of
room ids it can see into. While the train occupies that room, the same window it
runs on its own position runs again on each observed room, but distant: the
observed room hears "The subway passes in the distance." and the riders see "In
the distance: the platform." and glimpse anyone there "in the distance." The data
is local to the pass-through room, so the vehicle needs no map; it just reads the
`observe` list of wherever it happens to be. A room with no `observe` list is
unchanged, so slice 2a vehicles keep working.

### Verification

Shipped: `agent_vehicle.c` factors the window into `glimpse(self, room, distant)`
and `window` calls it on the occupied room and then, from that room's `observe`
list, on each room it can see into. A smoke test gives the tunnel a platform to
observe, rides through (the rider glimpses the platform in the distance), then
stands on the platform and sees the subway pass in the distance.

## Slice 2c: Elevators on a path (deferred)

An on-command vehicle that shows the floors it passes, walking its path toward
the requested stop instead of hopping. This needs a directional walk toward an
arbitrary target rather than the train's simple loop.

This is gated in the same spirit as the federation gate in `FUTURE.md`: build it
when routes are real and busy enough to make the extra work worth the code, not
before.

## Dependencies

Depends on: OLC-7 (the vehicle agent and the `board`/`disembark`/`floor`/`call`
verbs) and OLC-5 (the agent event loop this extends).
