# Milestone 47: A branching job chain (campaign arc)

Status: planned. It lets contracts depend on one another, so it is Milestone 47 in
the project sequence. M37 built jobs and M44 seeded a district's worth of them, but
every contract stands alone: a player can farm the one bounty and there is no
progression. This milestone adds the primitive that turns a scatter of jobs into an
arc a player works through.

## Goal

A job today is gated only by standing (`job_min`) and by a per-giver cooldown, and
nothing on the sheet records which jobs a character has finished, so no contract can
require another first. The district's work is therefore flat: take the bounty, turn
it in, repeat.

This milestone adds a named-flag primitive so a contract can require an earlier one
and grant a token on completion, and wires a short campaign arc into the district
from it. It is content-light in code, building on the existing gate-and-turnin
pattern, and it keeps the setting mundane and operator-facing, so it deepens the
game without new mechanics or growing the codebase much.

## Background: what is already in place

- **The job fields.** A giver carries `job=1`, a `job_desc`, a goal (`job_target`
  bounty or `job_dest` courier), rewards (`job_creds`, `job_cp`, `job_standing`), an
  optional `job_min` standing gate, and a `job_cd` cooldown. The sheet holds
  `job_giver` and `job_done`; `turnin` pays the reward and clears the slot.
- **The gate pattern.** `verb_jobs.c` reads `job_min` in two places, `show_offer`
  (which marks a locked contract in the `jobs` list) and `accept` (which refuses
  one). A prerequisite check slots in exactly beside it.
- **The comma-list idiom.** `maneuvers` (unlocks) and `standing` (faction steps) are
  comma-separated lists on the sheet, read by `cs_has_unlock` and written by
  `cs_standing_add`. A completion-flag list follows the same shape: a membership
  reader and an appender.
- **The turn-in seam.** `turnin` already writes the sheet, paying creds and CP and
  adding a `job_standing` step. Granting a completion flag is one more write in the
  same place.

## Design: named flags on the sheet

A giver may carry two new fields. `job_need=<flag>` gates the contract: it is offered
and accepted only when the character's sheet carries that flag. `job_grant=<flag>`
is the reward token: turning the contract in adds that flag to the sheet's `flags`
list. Named flags, rather than a record keyed by giver id, let several contracts
grant the same flag and let one contract need a flag any of them provide, so a
branch is expressible without new machinery. The gate mirrors `job_min`, and the
grant mirrors the `job_standing` reward, so the whole primitive is a reader, an
appender, and two checks on seams that already exist.

## Slice a: the prerequisite gate (`job_need`) (shipped)

The gating half.

- Add a `cs_has_flag(sheet, flag)` helper that reads the sheet's `flags` list, in the
  shape of `cs_has_unlock`.
- Read `job_need` beside `job_min` in `show_offer` (mark the contract locked, naming
  the prerequisite) and in `accept` (refuse it with the same message shape).

A smoke test drives a non-admin character against a giver whose contract carries a
`job_need`: `jobs` marks it locked and `accept` refuses it while the flag is absent,
and both open once the flag is set on the sheet.

Shipped: `cs_has_unlock` was refactored into a shared `cs_list_has(sheet, prop,
item)` membership reader in `chromesix_verb.h`, and `cs_has_flag(sheet, flag)` reads
the sheet's `flags` list through it. `verb_jobs.c` reads `job_need` in the two seams
that already read `job_min`: `show_offer` marks the contract "(locked: requires
<flag>)" and `accept` refuses it ("You are not cleared for that job yet (requires
<flag>)."), both only while the flag is absent from the sheet. A smoke test drives
non-admin TestPlayer3 against a giver whose contract carries `job_need`: `jobs` shows
it locked and `accept` refuses it while the flag is absent, and both open once the
flag is set on the sheet. The gate is proven on a non-admin because admin does not
mask a flag gate, but a player meets it only by carrying the flag.

## Slice b: the completion grant (`job_grant`) (shipped)

The granting half, which closes the loop.

- On `turnin`, append the giver's `job_grant` flag to the sheet's `flags`, in the
  shape of `cs_standing_add`, alongside the existing creds, CP, and standing payout.

A smoke test finishes a contract that carries a `job_grant`, confirms the flag lands
on the sheet, and then confirms a second contract that needs that flag, locked
before, now accepts.

Shipped: `cs_flag_add(sheet, flag)` in `chromesix_verb.h` appends a flag to the
`flags` list once (a flag already present is left as is, so a repeated turnin never
doubles it), the granting mirror of `cs_standing_add`. `turnin` reads the giver's
`job_grant` beside the `job_standing` payout and, when set, grants the flag and
prints "You are now cleared as <flag>.". A smoke test drives non-admin TestPlayer3
against two lobby givers, courierA (grants `keycard`) and courierB (needs it):
courierB is locked before, then TestPlayer3 turns courierA in (job_done set by admin
to isolate the grant from M37b completion), the flag lands on the sheet appended
after the arctoken from the slice-a test, and courierB now accepts.

## Slice c: a seeded campaign chain

Wire the primitive into playable content: a short arc in the waystation, authored as
seeded jobs, with no new geography.

- The existing fixer's scavenger bounty becomes step one, granting the first flag.
- A new contact in the district offers a follow-up gated on that flag and granting a
  second, for a larger reward.
- A final payoff contract needs the second flag and pays the arc's best reward.

The givers sit in existing district rooms, so this is content on the a and b
mechanics. A smoke test runs the whole arc end to end against the seeded ids:
completing each step unlocks the next (a later step is locked until its predecessor
is turned in), and the final reward lands.

## Deferred: true branches and repeatable arcs

Mutually exclusive paths (a choice that forecloses another), faction-forked
outcomes, repeatable chains, and campaign state richer than flags stay deferred as
later content, once the linear arc proves the primitive. The flag model already
admits a branch (several grants of one flag, or a need any grant satisfies); the
deferral is the authored content, not the mechanism.

## Verification

A smoke test drives the arc against the seeded ids: it confirms a `job_need`
contract is locked and refused without its flag and open with it, that `turnin`
grants a `job_grant` flag, and that the seeded chain gates each step behind the
previous through to the final reward. The gate and grant are proven on a non-admin
character, since standing and flag gates are not masked by admin but should still be
exercised the way a player meets them.

## Dependencies

Depends on: M37 (jobs, `accept`, and `turnin`, the seams this extends), M38 (the
`job_min` standing gate this mirrors and the comma-list idiom the flag list follows),
M44 (the district and its fixer that becomes step one of the arc), and M46 (the
respawning encounter a bounty step targets, so the chain is replayable).
