# Milestone 45: Close out the combat catalog

Status: planned. It lands the last Section 11 unlocks and conditions the earlier
combat milestones deferred, so it is Milestone 45 in the project sequence. After
the M25c-M42 arc built the engine and its reaction family, three catalog rows and
three conditions were left as explicit follow-ons. This milestone adds them, so
every family the rules lay out is proven end to end and `learn` and `use` have the
full shelf.

## Goal

Three deferrals remain from the combat arc. M42 held back the initiative and
marking unlocks (Wired Reflexes, Tactical Co-Processor, Threat-Assessment Optics)
because they act on your own turn rather than as reactions, and M40 held back three
conditions (Blinded, Held, and a persistent Exposed) to land with their inflictors
once the framework was proven. Both notes point at the same follow-on: the rows and
conditions exist as seams with nothing hung on them yet.

This milestone hangs them. No new mechanism is needed: each item is a catalog row
or a condition prop on a hook that already exists. It reuses the `cs_unlocks`
catalog and the `use` dispatch (M36), the M40 condition framework (a prop read
where it bites), the `cs_attack_resolve` hooks, and the opposed-roll inflictor
pattern from `trip`, `stun`, and `rend` (M40). It is a low-code, high-completeness
milestone that substantially finishes the ChromeSix combat catalog. In keeping
with the size constraint, it adds rows and small verbs on proven patterns, not new
systems.

## Background: what is already in place

- **The catalog and its dispatch.** `cs_unlocks[]` in `chromesix_verb.h` is one row
  per unlock (`{ id, cp, hook, graft, grit, effect }`). `learn` reads the cost and
  gates; a `UEF_PASSIVE` row is read where its stat applies, and an active row is
  dispatched in `verb_use.c` by its `UEF_` tag. `cs_has_unlock` reads the sheet's
  `maneuvers` list. Adding an unlock is a row plus, for an active one, a `use` case.
- **The initiative roll.** `verb_combat.c` rolls a Wit pool per combatant at combat
  open, sorts descending, and writes the order back so the roster order is the turn
  order for the fight. A turn-order unlock reads at that one loop.
- **The condition framework (M40).** A condition is a prop on the sheet, named by
  `cs_cond_list` and read where it bites: `cs_attack_resolve` for pool and Passive
  Defense shifts, the turn grant for action denial, `cs_cond_tick` for per-turn
  effects. Prone, Stunned, Shaken, Bleeding, and Suppressed already follow it.
- **The inflictor pattern.** `trip`, `stun`, `menace`, and `rend` are small verbs
  that make an opposed Might-plus-Brawl (or Charm-plus-Command) roll and set a
  condition prop on the loser; `stand` and `staunch` clear one. A new condition
  arrives with a verb of the same shape.
- **Exposed, transiently.** `verb_ambush.c` already applies Exposed as a `pd_bonus`
  of -2 passed into a single `cs_attack_resolve` call, so the effect exists but is
  not a tracked state a later attack can read.

## Design: rows and props on existing seams

Each unlock is a `cs_unlocks` row. A turn-order unlock is read at the initiative
loop; a marking or debuff unlock is an active row (the M41b offensive pattern,
`use <unlock> <target>`) that sets a tracked condition on its target. Each new
condition is a prop added to `cs_cond_list` and read at the one point it bites, set
by an inflictor unlock or a small verb built on the `trip` shape. Nothing new is
introduced at the resolution or the turn loop; the milestone fills in the reads and
the rows those seams were built to carry.

## Slice a: the initiative unlock (Wired Reflexes)

The first unlock that touches turn order rather than a hit.

- **Wired Reflexes** (cyberware, cyber graft, passive): a combatant with the mod
  adds bonus dice to its Wit initiative roll, so it tends to act earlier in the
  round.

Plan: one `cs_unlocks` row (`wired`, cyber graft, `UEF_PASSIVE` so `use` refuses
it) and a `cs_has_unlock` read in the initiative loop of `verb_combat.c`, adding a
fixed bonus to the combatant's rolled initiative before the sort. This is the first
unlock read at the turn-order seam rather than in the resolution or `cs_recalc`. A
smoke test builds two combatants of equal Wit, one with the mod and a large enough
bonus to be deterministic against the roll variance, opens a fight, and reads the
written roster to confirm the wired combatant leads the order.

## Slice b: the marking and debuff unlocks with Exposed

Two on-your-turn active unlocks, each applying a tracked condition to a foe, and
the M40-deferred persistent Exposed as a real state.

- **Tactical Co-Processor** (cyberware, cyber graft, Grit + action): applies
  **Exposed** (a persistent -2 Passive Defense) to a target, the debuff. Exposed
  becomes a tracked `exposed` prop read in `cs_attack_resolve` and listed by
  `cs_cond_list`, replacing the transient ambush-only form.
- **Threat-Assessment Optics** (cyberware, cyber graft, Grit + action): **marks** a
  target so the marker's own attacks against it gain a die until the mark clears, a
  `marked_by` prop read in `cs_attack_resolve` when the attacker is the marker.

Plan: two `cs_unlocks` rows and two new `UEF_` tags dispatched in `verb_use.c` on
the M41b offensive target pattern, plus two reads in `cs_attack_resolve` (the
defender's `exposed` lowers its Passive Defense; the attacker's mark on this
defender raises the pool). Exposed also joins `cs_cond_list`, and `verb_ambush.c`
can set the tracked prop rather than only pass the transient bonus. A smoke test
applies each unlock to a built foe and confirms, in the resolution line, the
Passive Defense drop from Exposed and the pool gain from a mark the attacker owns.

## Slice c: the remaining conditions (Blinded and Held)

The last two M40 conditions, each a prop plus a hook plus a small inflictor.

- **Blinded** (`blind` prop): a heavy penalty on the blinded combatant's own
  attacks, read in `cs_attack_resolve` and shown by `cs_cond_list`. Inflictor: a
  `flash` maneuver on the `stun` shape.
- **Held** (`held` prop): the held combatant cannot move (a hook in the `go` and
  band-change path) and acts at a penalty. Inflictor: a `grapple` maneuver, an
  opposed Might-plus-Brawl roll like `trip`; `break` (or `stand`) ends it.

Plan: two condition props, their reads in `cs_attack_resolve` and the movement
path, their lines in `cs_cond_list`, and two inflictor verbs added to `verbs.conf`
on the established pattern. A smoke test inflicts each on a built foe and confirms
the attack penalty for Blinded and, for Held, that the held combatant's `go` is
refused until it breaks free.

## Deferred: pure-content rows and living-world respawn

The Section 11 rows that add no new family (further spells and cyberware that only
adjust a stat already read somewhere) are trivial follow-on rows on these same
patterns and are left as content. NPC respawn for the M44 starter district, which
needs a reset rule scoped so it does not disturb the global `@reset` counts the OLC
tests assert, stays open as a later content and world milestone.

## Verification

A smoke test drives each addition end to end against a built foe in an isolated
room, the established pattern. It stacks the pools so the outcome is deterministic:
it confirms Wired Reflexes leads the initiative order, that Tactical Co-Processor
lowers a target's Passive Defense through a tracked Exposed and Threat-Assessment
Optics raises the marker's pool against a marked target, and that Blinded penalizes
the blinded combatant's attack while Held refuses its movement until it breaks
free. The combat parts re-send on a fast poll so an action lands inside the turn
window, and down the foe with a `fight_over` teardown.

## Dependencies

Depends on: M25c (the attack verb and `cs_attack_resolve` these rows and conditions
read and write), M25d (the Grit gauge and the action economy the active unlocks
spend), M35 and M36 (the CP economy and the table-driven unlock catalog the rows
join), M40 (the condition framework and the inflictor-verb pattern the conditions
follow), and M41 and M42 (the active and reaction families that proved the
`use`-target and passive-read patterns these rows reuse).
