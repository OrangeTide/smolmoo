# Milestone 50: Test-driver robustness for chained combat and social scenes

Status: planned. It finishes the determinism work M46 through M49 began, so it is Milestone
50 in the project sequence. M49 hardened the harness waits (readiness and outcome), and M49d
fixed two isolated scene reads. What M49d could not fix was the M25c combat and M25e social
section, where scenes chain through shared state and a point fix to one scene cascades into
its neighbours. This milestone reworks how that section drives the turn engine.

## Goal

The M25c and M25e section drives an asynchronous, turn-based combat and social engine over
HTTP and SSE with ad-hoc per-scene loops. Each scene rolls its own retry: attack a foe until
a line appears, hold until a prompt shows, send a social command until an outcome. Three
weaknesses make the section flaky under load, and M49d proved they resist point fixes.

First, an action only lands on the player's turn. `attack`, `flee`, `retreat`, `use`, and a
social push are all refused off-turn ("It is not your turn."), and a bare send that happens to
fall in the roughly one second of NPC turns between the player's thirty-second turns does
nothing. The scene then asserts on an event that never happened.

Second, the scenes chain through one shared `cb_active` flag (combat and social share it, so
the two modes are mutually exclusive). A scene that does not fully tear down leaves `cb_active`
set, so the next scene's opener draws its foe into the dying scene, or a social scene is
refused, and the failure cascades through every later scene until something resets. One flaky
turn near the top of the chain can fail a dozen downstream checks.

Third, `test.sh` runs under `set -e`, so a helper that returns nonzero on a timeout kills the
whole run rather than failing one check. A driver primitive has to be safe against that.

This milestone replaces the ad-hoc loops with a small set of shared, deterministic primitives,
so an action is only sent on the player's turn, a scene is confirmed idle before the next one
opens, and one flaky moment fails at most its own check instead of cascading. The suite stays
at 454 checks; this hardens the drivers, it does not add features.

## Background: what is already in place

- **The scene section.** M25c is a chain of combat scenes in one player session: an enforcer,
  a gunner at range, a tough brute, a multi-foe frontage fight (three gangers), an ambush from
  stealth, a flee across rooms, and a span-0 vault. M25e is a chain of social scenes (an
  informant, a broker, a menace) on the same engine with `cb_mode` set to "social". Each scene
  rebuilds or reuses the character and runs against seeded or throwaway foes.
- **The driver helpers.** `fight_over FOE...` drives a fight to a confirmed teardown by
  attacking the named foes and probing with `hold`, which replies "not in a fight" only once
  `__combat` has cleared `cb_active` (an outcome counted as a delta). `social_drive CMD
  OUTCOME` sends a social command and polls for a fresh outcome before resending. Both are
  called `|| true` so a timeout does not trip `set -e`. Outside these two, each scene has its
  own inline retry loop.
- **The turn engine.** `verb_combat.c` runs the round loop. A foe is drawn into a fight only on
  the player's turn (`verb_attack.c` prints "It is not your turn." and exits otherwise); the
  player's turn blocks for `TIMEOUT_MS` (thirty seconds) waiting for an action, and a second
  action the same turn is refused ("You have already acted this turn."). Opening a fight prints
  "You move on the FOE -- combat begins!"; a foe drawn into an existing fight is reported by
  `cs_attack_resolve` as "<player> attacks FOE". The player's turn prompt lists each live foe
  as "FOE at <band> range". `cb_active`, `cb_turn`, and `cb_roster` are string props on the
  room.
- **The prop endpoint.** `GET /prop?obj=<id>&prop=<name>&sid=<sid>` returns a string prop's
  value on any object, given a live SSE session id, without racing the SSE log. The suite
  already reads room state this way (the `@gc` test reads `cb_roster`). This is the
  deterministic source of truth for turn and scene state.
- **The stress mode.** `make stress STRESS_N=N` (M49c) runs the suite N times against one build
  and fails on the first failing run, the evidence tool for this milestone.

## Design: a few shared, turn-aware primitives read from the source of truth

The rework centres on reading the engine's own turn and scene state through `GET /prop`, which
is synchronous and does not race the SSE log, rather than inferring it from log lines or by
spamming `hold` (the approach M49d tried as `await_idle`, which destabilised scenes by yielding
the player's turns). The room whose state is read is the player's current location, captured
once per scene the way the suite already captures a character id.

- **A turn and idle check.** Read `cb_active` and `cb_turn` on the combat room by `GET /prop`.
  A scene is idle when `cb_active` is not 1; it is the player's turn when `cb_turn` names the
  player. These are the gates the primitives wait on.
- **`on_turn CMD OUTCOME`.** Send a turn-bound action (attack, flee, retreat, use, a social
  push) only when the prop check says it is the player's turn, then poll for a fresh OUTCOME.
  If the turn passes before OUTCOME appears, wait for the next player turn and resend. This one
  primitive replaces the scattered per-action loops and removes the off-turn refusal flake.
- **`open_fight FOE`.** Confirm the previous scene is idle (the prop check), then open a fresh
  fight and wait for "combat begins", so a foe is never drawn into a scene that is still
  tearing down. This removes the cascade at scene boundaries.
- **Teardown that is actually confirmed.** Keep `fight_over` and `social_drive`, but pair a
  scene's teardown with the prop-based idle check so a `|| true` timeout is not silently
  followed by the next opener. The idle check reads state rather than driving the fight, so it
  cannot yield the player's turns the way a hold probe does.

Every primitive is safe under `set -e`: it returns a status the caller can ignore with `||
true`, and never leaves the script on a nonzero return. The principle is that a driver waits on
what the engine actually reports about itself, not on a guess about timing, so a slow machine
waits longer and a correct scene still passes.

## Slice a: the turn-aware primitives and the state read

Build and prove the primitives in isolation before touching the scenes.

- Add the `GET /prop` reads of `cb_active` and `cb_turn` on the combat room, and the helper
  that captures the room id (the player's location) for a scene.
- Add `on_turn` and `open_fight` over those reads, each `set -e` safe.
- Unit-test the primitives against a live scene the way M49a tested the readiness helpers: a
  turn that is not yet the player's is waited out, an action lands on the player's turn, and a
  never-idle scene returns a status rather than killing the run.

## Slice b: convert the combat chain

Move the M25c combat scenes onto the primitives.

- Replace each scene's inline opener and action loops (enforcer, gunner, brute, multi-foe and
  frontage, ambush, flee, vault) with `open_fight` and `on_turn`, and gate each scene start on
  a confirmed idle.
- Restore the frontage check that M49d had to pull back out, now that its neighbours no longer
  bleed through `cb_active`.
- Confirm with `make stress` that a flaky turn in one scene no longer cascades into the next.

## Slice c: convert the social chain and prove it

Move the M25e social scenes onto the primitives and make the determinism checkable.

- Convert the informant, broker, and menace scenes to `on_turn` and the idle gate.
- Run a long `make stress` streak, fully clean, as the evidence, where the section previously
  cascaded roughly once in thirty runs.
- Document the driver pattern (a scene opens on confirmed idle and acts only on the player's
  turn, both read from `GET /prop`) alongside the M49 timing model in `README.md`, and update
  the turn-loop test-pattern note.

## Deferred: the isolated later scenarios

The combat and social scenarios after M25 (the M40-M45 maneuver scenes, the agent and history
instances) run against their own throwaway foes and do not chain through a shared teardown, so
they are not part of this rework. M49d already fixed the two that flaked there. Converting them
to the same primitives is optional polish, not a source of cascades, and stays deferred unless
one is shown to flake.

## Verification

A long `make stress` streak runs fully clean where the M25c and M25e section previously
cascaded, a healthy run's time is unchanged (the prop reads return at once), and the frontage
check that M49d deferred passes as part of the converted chain. The suite still reports 454
checks, since this milestone hardens the drivers rather than adding features.

## Dependencies

Depends on: the smoke-test harness and its M49 waits (`poll`, `check_log`, `wait_ready`), the
combat and social engine whose `cb_active`, `cb_turn`, and prompt lines the primitives read
(M25c, M25e), and the `GET /prop` endpoint the reads go through. It completes the M46-M49
determinism thread and unblocks the frontage check deferred from M49d.
