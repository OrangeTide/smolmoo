# Milestone 44: A playable starter district

Status: shipped (slices a, b, c). It seeds the first cohesive playable area, so it
is Milestone 44 in the project sequence. After the combat engine and its polish, the systems still
have nowhere to happen: this milestone builds one small district that ties chargen,
movement, jobs, standing, combat, and the services into a loop a new character can
actually play.

## Goal

The world seed carries seven rooms (several are test duplicates), fifteen NPCs of
which one has a faction and none a job, and a scatter of items. The jobs, factions,
clinic, vendor, and combat-encounter systems are exercised only by test setup, not
by any world a player can walk into. A freshly created character lands in the lobby
with a rich rule set and nothing to do.

This milestone closes that gap with a starter district: a handful of connected
rooms with the services in place, a fixer offering a real contract, a foe
encounter that repopulates, and a faction whose standing bites. It is mostly world
content, which is data rather than source, so it validates the engine as a game
without growing the codebase. It also shakes out integration the isolated tests
cannot, since those build their own objects while this drives the seeded ones.

## Background: what is already in place

- **The systems.** Jobs (M37), standing and disposition (M38), in-game time and the
  clinic and short rest (M39), combat (M25c and the M40-M42 arc), the vendor
  (M25's store), movement, and the status and help polish (M43) are all built and
  tested. Nothing here needs a new mechanic; it needs seeded content that uses
  them.
- **The seed formats.** A room is a child of `#100` with a `name`, a
  `description`, and flags read where they matter: `vendor=<npc>` names a vendor,
  `safe=1` allows a short rest, `clinic=1` allows `treat`. An exit is a named
  object in the room carrying a `dest` room id, matched by the word a player types
  after `go`. An NPC is a child of `#200` with `grade`, `wielded`, and the optional
  `faction`, `disp`, `brain`, and job fields (`job=1`, `job_desc`, `job_creds`,
  `job_cp`, `job_dest`, `job_fac`, `job_min`, `job_cd`). An item is a child of
  `#300` with its combat or gear props and a price for vendor stock.
- **Reset rules.** A rule is a child of `#910` naming a `room`, a `proto`, and a
  `count`; `@reset` tops each room up to that many live children of the proto,
  idempotently, so a district's foes and loot repopulate after they are cleared.
  No reset instances exist yet, so encounters do not currently respawn.
- **Areas.** A child of `#900` groups rooms for shared ownership and for the
  `@reset [#area]` filter, so a district can be reset as a unit.

## Design: a district as seeded data

The district is authored entirely as objects in the world seed, using the formats
above, in a reserved id block that does not collide with the dynamic ids the tests
allocate through `@create`. It is deliberately small: a hub of a few rooms with the
services, one job site reachable from it, and one faction presence. The theme
stays mundane and operator-facing, a working frontier waystation, so it introduces
the setting without touching the GM-only cosmology. Any engine change is only what
integration surfaces when the seeded objects are driven for real; the default is
content.

## Slice a: the hub and its services (shipped)

A small hub of connected rooms with the essentials in place:

- A concourse (the arrival room and travel junction), a bar or office (where the
  fixer will stand), a clinic room, a shop room with a `vendor` NPC stocking a few
  priced items, and a bunk room (`safe=1`) for a short rest. Exits connect them
  both ways.

Shipped: a five-room waystation seeded in `world.data` in a reserved id block
(rooms `#120`-`#124`, exits `#130`-`#139`, vendor `#220`, clinic device and stock
`#320`-`#322`). The concourse `#120` links to the lobby `#101` and out to the bar,
med bay, supply, and bunks, each with a return exit. Supply names a `clerk` vendor
(`vendor=220`) holding a priced vest and blade and a `stim` counter price; the med
bay holds an `autodoc` (`clinic=1`, `treat_fee=40`); the bunks carry `safe=1` for a
short rest. A smoke test walks in from the lobby, lists and buys from the seeded
vendor, treats a wound at the seeded clinic (confirmed by the wound count dropping,
not just the message, so it cannot pass on an earlier clinic's log line), and rests
in the seeded bunk to recover BP, all against the fixed seed ids.

Two integration notes the walkthrough surfaced. A world-seed line beginning with
`#` is parsed as an object header, so the seed carries no comment lines. And
movement is blocked when carry load exceeds capacity, which is Might-derived, so
the walkthrough restores a workable Might first (an earlier test had zeroed it).

## Slice b: the fixer and a job chain (shipped)

The playable loop:

- A fixer NPC in the hub carrying a real contract (`job=1` with a description and
  creds, CP, and a faction step as reward), a job site room reachable through the
  hub's exits, and a foe encounter there. The bounty completes on the scene win
  and pays out at turn-in, the M37 loop, now from the seed.

Shipped: the Cargo Bay job site `#125` and its exits (`#140` in from the concourse,
`#141` back out), the fixer `#222` in The Long Haul carrying the contract
(`job=1`, `job_desc` "Clear the scavenger out of the cargo bay.",
`job_target=scavenger`, `job_creds=120`, `job_cp=2`, `job_standing=dockers:1`), and
the scavenger foe `#221` (`grade=tough`, `mig=9`). A smoke test drives the seeded
ids end to end: it accepts the fixer's contract in the bar, travels to the cargo
bay, defeats the scavenger (polling the attack until `job_done` flips), returns,
turns in, and confirms the 120 creds, 2 CP, and `dockers:1` standing all land.

One integration decision the walkthrough forced. The scavenger is hand-placed in
the cargo bay (`location=#125`) rather than stocked by a `#910` reset rule. A
seeded reset rule changes the global `@reset` counts, and the pre-existing OLC tests
assert those counts exactly, so a permanent rule broke them. Hand-placing the
encounter keeps the playable loop intact and leaves the OLC tests untouched.
Reset-based respawn, so the foe repopulates after it is cleared, is deferred to a
later slice or milestone that can revisit the reset-count tests.

## Slice c: a faction presence and a living NPC (shipped)

The district reacts to who the player is:

- A faction seeded on the district's NPCs so standing has a consequence here: a
  faction-gated vendor or job (`job_min`), or a mob that turns hostile at low
  standing through `cs_disposition`, and a roving or reactive NPC (a `brain`) so
  the district is not static.

Shipped: a Dockers Office `#126` off the concourse (exits `#142`/`#143`) holding
two seeded NPCs, both `faction=dockers`. The handler `#223` offers a courier
contract (`job_dest=125`, 90 creds, 1 CP, `dockers:1`) gated behind
`job_min=dockers:1`, so a fresh character sees it locked and is refused until the
slice-b bounty earns the band, after which it accepts and pays out. The guard
`#224` (`behavior=greet,patrol`, a `greeting`, `route=126,120`, `brain=#453`)
reacts to who enters: it greets a newcomer, aggros a dockers-Hostile one through
`cs_disposition` with no aggro token, and roves its route when woken as an agent.

A smoke test drives the seeded ids for each consequence: it enters the office and
sees the guard greet, reads the handler's contract as locked and is refused at
Neutral, earns `dockers:1` and then accepts, runs the courier leg and turns it in
for the reward, wakes the guard and confirms it roves (then sleeps it so a
boot-scan does not re-wake it), and re-enters at Hostile standing to draw the
guard's aggro.

The faction gate reads through the giver's disposition, so the same `disp` offset
that lets a fixer vouch for you (M38c) also lets a handler. The guard is left
unwoken in the seed, reactive through its `on_enter` path, and only the test wakes
it, so the district does not rove on its own until a builder wakes it.

## Deferred: a larger world and a campaign arc

Further districts, a branching job tree, and a multi-session campaign arc are the
larger content and systems efforts that follow once one district proves the seed
patterns. They stay deferred; this milestone is one self-contained slice of world,
not a sprawl.

## Verification

A smoke test drives the whole starter loop against the seeded ids: it walks the
hub, uses the vendor, clinic, and safe room, takes the fixer's contract, fights the
reset-spawned foe at the job site, turns it in for the reward, and confirms the
faction gate. Driving the fixed seed rather than test-built objects is the point,
since it exercises the integration the isolated tests never touch. The combat parts
poll for the scene outcome before the next command, the established pattern.

## Dependencies

Depends on: M37 (jobs and contracts), M38 (standing and disposition), M39 (the
clinic and short rest), the M25c-M42 combat arc (the encounter), M32-M33 (the OLC
building and reset machinery the district is authored with), and M43 (the status,
help, and onboarding a new player meets on arrival).
